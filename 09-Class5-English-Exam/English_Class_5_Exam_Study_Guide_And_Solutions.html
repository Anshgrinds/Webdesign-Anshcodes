SOLUTIONS • SECTION A: READING COMPREHENSION 8 Marks
Q1 (a) Grandfather's treatment of the tiger (2 Marks)
Model Answer: Grandfather put both his arms through the iron railings of the cage, stroked the tiger's forehead, tickled its ears, and playfully smacked him across the mouth whenever he gave a low growl.
Marking Criteria: 1 mark for mentioning stroking/tickling ears; 1 mark for mentioning smacking across the mouth or arms through railings.
Q1 (b) Reason for the tiger springing back (1 Mark)
Model Answer: The tiger sprang back only when a noisy leopard trapped in the adjacent cage snarled loudly at him.
Marking Criteria: Full 1 mark for identifying the snarling leopard next door.
Q1 (c) Shocking revelation by the zoo keeper (2 Marks)
Model Answer: The zoo keeper revealed that this tiger was not Timothy; Timothy had died of pneumonia two months earlier. This particular beast was a dangerous, wild tiger trapped in the hills only the previous month.
Marking Criteria: 1 mark for Timothy's death from pneumonia; 1 mark for explaining that this tiger was a fierce wild beast from the hills.
Q1 (d) Vocabulary in Context (2 Marks)
(i) Great pleasure or enjoyment: relish (1 Mark)
(ii) Next to or adjoining: adjacent (1 Mark)
Q1 (e) Antonym of "fierce" (1 Mark)
Model Answer: Gentle / mild / tame / peaceful (Any valid antonym receives 1 mark).

Q2. Reference to Context (4 Marks)
Part (a): "Goodnight, Timothy... and may you live happily here."
(i) Spoken by Grandfather. (1 Mark)
(ii) Spoken to the tiger in the zoo cage. Grandfather said this as he bade farewell, thinking he was saying goodbye to his beloved childhood pet Timothy before the zookeeper startled him with the truth. (1 Mark)

Part (b): "Take this goat, but remember never to strike it or utter false words in its presence!"
(i) Spoken by the generous Giant to the honest poor boy/farmer. (1 Mark)
(ii) The magic goat had the miraculous power to produce gold/silver coins and abundant nourishment whenever spoken to with truth and respect. (1 Mark)

Q4. Short Answer Questions — Literature Chapters (8 Marks)
(a) Grandpa Tickles a Tiger (2 Marks):
Grandfather brought Timothy home after discovering the helpless, shivering cub abandoned in the Terai jungle during a hunting expedition. The family sent him to Lucknow Zoo six months later because Timothy had grown into a full-sized tiger, became increasingly aggressive, began stalking the household cat and poultry, and Grandfather feared he might harm Mahmoud the cook.

(b) A Giant and His Magic Goat (2 Marks):
The dishonest innkeeper swapped the magic goat with an ordinary-looking goat while the boy was asleep at the inn. The boy discovered the deceit when the replacement goat failed to produce treasures, returned to the giant for counsel, and used the giant's enchanted walking stick to compel the thief to return the true magic goat.

(c) The Locked Room (2 Marks):
The room was kept locked because it contained delicate, antique family inventions and mechanical clockwork automata crafted by an eccentric ancestor. When the children finally unlocked the brass door, they discovered not a ghost or monster, but a wondrous hidden workshop filled with gleaming brass gears, musical clockwork toys, and forgotten memories.

(d) Workbook Reflection (2 Marks):
The boy demonstrates honesty, respect, humility, and courage. Instead of being greedy or terrified of the giant's immense size, he treats the giant with courtesy and keeps his promises, proving that character matters more than physical stature.

Q6. Verb Form Sentence Blanks (4 Marks)
a. Grandfather has known (know) the tiger cub since it was only eighteen inches long. (Present perfect requires V3: known)
b. Yesterday, the boy brought (bring) the magic goat to his village cottage. (Past time marker 'yesterday' requires V2: brought)
c. While the wind was howling outside, the old door began to creak or creaking (creak) loudly. (Infinitive or gerund after 'began')
d. The mysterious key was found (find) inside an antique rosewood drawer. (Passive voice was + V3: found)
Q7. Reported Speech Transformations (6 Marks)
a. Direct: Grandfather said, "I know this tiger very well."
Indirect: Grandfather said that he knew that tiger very well. (1.5 Marks)
Rules: 'said' remains 'said that'; pronoun 'I' becomes 'he'; present 'know' shifts to past 'knew'; 'this' shifts to 'that'.

b. Direct: The keeper said to Grandfather, "Your Timothy died two months ago."
Indirect: The keeper told Grandfather that his Timothy had died two months before (or earlier). (1.5 Marks)
Rules: 'said to' becomes 'told'; pronoun 'your' becomes 'his'; simple past 'died' shifts to past perfect 'had died'; 'ago' becomes 'before'.

c. Direct: The giant said, "Do not betray my trust."
Indirect: The giant warned (or instructed) him not to betray his trust. (1.5 Marks)
Rules: Imperative negative 'Do not' becomes 'not to + verb'; pronoun 'my' becomes 'his'.

d. Direct: Mother asked Rohan, "Did you see anyone unlock that room?"
Indirect: Mother asked Rohan if (or whether) he had seen anyone unlock that room. (1.5 Marks)
Rules: Yes/No question uses 'if/whether'; 'Did you see' (simple past) shifts to past perfect 'he had seen'.
Q8. Error Identification & Correction in Reported Speech (3 Marks)
a. Error: "is reading... yesterday"
Corrected: Ravi said that he was reading Ruskin Bond's story the day before (or the previous day). (1 Mark)

b. Error: "told to Grandfather"
Corrected: The zookeeper told Grandfather that the tiger was dangerous. (Note: 'told' takes a direct object without 'to'). (1 Mark)

c. Error: "that if"
Corrected: He asked me if I had found the brass key. (Note: Never use 'that' before 'if/whether' in reported questions). (1 Mark)
SOLUTIONS • SECTION D: CREATIVE WRITING MODEL ANSWERS 8 Marks
Model Option A: Diary Entry (Grandfather's Reflections)
14th October, 2026
Dehradun Home — 9:30 PM

Dear Diary,
A shudder still runs down my spine when I recall today's extraordinary occurrence at the Lucknow Zoo. I had marched confidently to what I believed was Timothy's enclosure. Feeling an overwhelming rush of affection, I stroked his whiskers, tickled his ears, and even tapped his jaw as I used to do back home.

Imagine my frozen horror when the breathless keeper revealed that my sweet Timothy had succumbed to pneumonia two months ago, and that this magnificent beast was a ferocious, wild maneater trapped in the hills only last month! How miraculously gentle that wild creature remained while licking my fingertips! It seemed as though, for a fleeting moment, kindness transcended even the fierce nature of a wild predator. A day of terror, yet one of profound wonder.
— Grandfather
Model Option B: The Locked Room (Climax & Revelation)
With trembling fingers, Maya inserted the heavy brass key into the ornate keyhole. With a prolonged, screeching creak, the stiff oak door swung inward. Dust danced in the amber shafts of afternoon light slicing through cracked shutters. Expecting a sinister creature or ancient spiderwebs, the children held their breath. Instead, their eyes widened in speechless astonishment. Before them sat an enchanted gallery of brass clockwork birds, miniature steam locomotives, and porcelain figures that softly chimed as the floorboards vibrated. Grandfather’s forgotten inventor's workshop had finally reawakened, banishing all their childhood fears into pure delight.
