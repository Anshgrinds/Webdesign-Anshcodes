SECTION B: SHORT ANSWER QUESTIONS [20 Marks]
Q1. What is a social organization? Write the names of any two social organizations active in our community.
Model Answer:
A social organization is a group of dedicated people formed not for personal profit, but to solve common community problems and help needy people.
Examples: 1. Nepal Red Cross Society • 2. Nepal Scouts (or Community Mother’s Group / Youth Club)
Q2. Mention any three causes of conflict among children in school.
Model Answer:
1. Unfair play: Cheating or not taking turns while playing games.
2. Teasing: Calling each other bad names or mocking classmates.
3. Disagreements: Fighting over classroom benches, books, or sports gear.
Q3. Write down four steps to resolve a misunderstanding between two friends.
Model Answer:
1. Stop and Cool Down: Avoid shouting or getting angry.
2. Listen Actively: Give both sides equal time to explain their feelings.
3. Find the Solution: Discuss peacefully and admit mistakes if wrong.
4. Shake Hands / Forgive: Say sorry, compromise, or take help from a teacher.
Q4. Differentiate between 'Rights' and 'Duties' with one example of each.
Model Answer:
• Rights: These are the legal and natural opportunities provided to citizens (or children) to grow and live safely. Example: The right to go to school and receive quality education.
• Duties: These are the responsibilities that we must perform for our family, school, and country. Example: Keeping the school compound clean and obeying traffic rules.
Q5. Write any three main functions of the District Coordination Committee (DCC).
Model Answer:
1. Coordination: It acts as a bridge between the provincial government and local municipalities / rural municipalities.
2. Monitoring Development: It supervises and inspects public projects like roads, bridges, and drinking water facilities.
3. Cooperation: It settles disputes and balances development work between neighboring municipalities in the district.
Q6. What is National Heritage? Mention its two main types with examples.
Model Answer:
National heritage includes ancient monuments, art, traditions, languages, and natural wonders inherited from the past that belong to the whole nation.
• 1. Cultural Heritage: Man-made sites and monuments (e.g., Pashupatinath Temple, Swayambhunath, Lumbini).
• 2. Natural Heritage: Gifts of nature (e.g., Mount Everest, Chitwan National Park, Rara Lake).
SECTION C: CREATIVE & VALUE-BASED QUESTIONS [15 Marks]
Q1. "Heritage sites are our national pride." Write four things you should NEVER do when visiting a heritage site.
Model Answer:
1. Never write graffiti, carve names, or scratch temple walls or statues.
2. Never litter plastic bottles, wrappers, or leftover food outside trash bins.
3. Never touch ancient paintings, delicate wood carvings, or museum exhibits.
4. Never use flash photography where signs say it is prohibited.
Q2. Situation-based Question:

"Two boys, Rohan and Bikram, are fighting during recess over who gets to bat first in cricket. Rohan pushes Bikram, and Bikram starts crying."

a) What was the cause of the conflict?
Impatience and disagreement over rules (who bats first) and lack of turn-taking.

b) How could this situation have been handled properly without fighting?
They should have tossed a coin to decide fairly, agreed on equal batting time, or asked a peer umpire to make a fair call rather than pushing.
