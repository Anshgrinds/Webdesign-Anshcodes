1. Nepali Literature & Eminent Poets

Poet / Writer Honorific Title Famous Literary Works Key Historical Fact
Bhanubhakta Acharya Aadikavi (First Poet) Ramayana (Bhanubhaktiya Ramayan) Simplified classical Sanskrit Ramayana into rhythmic, everyday Nepali metered verse (Chhanda).
Laxmi Prasad Devkota Mahakavi (Great Poet) Muna Madan, Shakuntala, Sulochana Wrote Muna Madan in Jhyaure folk meter; composed the 24-canto epic Shakuntala in only 3 months.
Lekhnath Paudyal Kabi Shiromani Rituvichar, Tarun Tapasi, Buddhibinod Pioneer of classical modern Nepali poetry; emphasized moral philosophy and beauty of nature.
Madhav Prasad Ghimire Rashtrakavi (National Poet) Gauri, Malati Mangale, Shakuntala Renowned patriotic poet of independent Nepal; lived past age 100 with enduring literary contributions.
Parijat (Bishnu Kumari) Celebrated Novelist Shiris ko Phool (The Blue Mimosa) First woman to win the prestigious Madan Puraskar (1965) for Shiris ko Phool.
Byakul Maila (P.K. Rai) National Anthem Lyricist Sayaun Thunga Phoolka (Hami) Written in 2006 to represent Nepal's unity in diversity, composed musically by Ambar Gurung.

2. Culture, Ethnicity, Music & Cinema

Community / Category Festivals & Traditional Dances Instruments & Cultural Attributes
Sherpa Gyalpo Lhosar (Tibetan New Year), Mani Rimdu festival. Dwell in Solukhumbu Himalayas; world-famous mountaineers; play Tungna.
Tharu Maghi (Harvest festival celebrated on 1st Magh), Jitiya. Indigenous inhabitants of the Terai; celebrate with Maghi dance & Dhakiya crafts.
Newar Indra Jatra, Gai Jatra, Mha Puja (worship of self). Original inhabitants of Kathmandu Valley; renowned for woodcarving, Lakhey dance & Dhime drum.
Tamang & Gurung Sonam Lhosar (Tamang) and Tamu Lhosar (Gurung); Ghatu dance. Tamang play the circular Damphu frame drum; Gurung famous for Rodhi cultural gatherings.
Folk Musical Instruments Madal: Two-sided leather drum; Sarangi: 4-string bowed lute played by Gandharvas; Bansuri: Bamboo flute. Core of Nepali folk melodies, folk songs (Lok Geet), and festival processions.
Nepali Films & Music Satya Harishchandra (1951 - 1st Nepali film made in India); Aama (1964 - 1st made inside Nepal); Kumari (1st Color film). Narayan Gopal: Swor Samrat (Emperor of Voice); Tara Devi: Swor Kinnari (over 4,000 recorded songs).

PART 2: Complete Examination Solutions & Model Answers Score: 50 / 50 Marks
SECTION A: Nepali Literature & Eminent Poets [6 Marks]
a) Bhanubhakta Acharya — Earned the title 'Aadikavi' for his accessible translation of the Ramayana from Sanskrit to Nepali.
b) Muna Madan — Laxmi Prasad Devkota's famous tragedy poem depicting Madan's journey to Lhasa and Muna's sacrifice.
c) Laxmi Prasad Devkota — Known as 'Mahakavi' (The Great Poet) for writing spontaneous masterpieces like Shakuntala.
d) Lekhnath Paudyal — Honored as 'Kabi Shiromani' for his profound moral and aesthetic contributions in classical verse.
e) Parijat — Celebrated novelist and writer who created the renowned Nepali novel 'Shiris ko Phool'.
f) Byakul Maila — Pen name of Pradeep Kumar Rai, writer of the current national anthem lyrics.
Teacher's Tip: Remind the student that 'Aadikavi' means the first or foundational poet, while 'Mahakavi' means the greatest poet.
SECTION B: Culture, Ethnicity & Festivals [6 Marks]
1. Sherpa Community ➔ C (High-altitude mountain dwellers celebrated for mountaineering & Gyalpo Lhosar).
2. Newar Community ➔ E (Rich heritage of Kathmandu Valley; celebrates Indra Jatra and Mha Puja).
3. Tharu Community ➔ A (Celebrates Maghi as their primary New Year & harvesting festival).
4. Kirat (Rai & Limbu) ➔ F (Nature worshippers celebrating Udhauli and Ubhauli with Chandi dance).
5. Chhath Parva ➔ B (Worshippers of the Sun God (Surya) along river banks for peace and health).
6. Tihar (Yama Panchak) ➔ D (Deepawali celebration honoring crows, dogs, cows, oxen, Goddess Laxmi, and brothers).
SECTION C: Music, Films & Traditional Instruments [6 Marks]

SECTION E: Science - True or False Challenge [8 Marks]
1. True (T) — The Sun is indeed a star that provides heat and light at the center of the solar system.
2. False (F) — Sound travels faster through dense matter (steel: ~5,000 m/s; air: only ~343 m/s).
3. True (T) — Chlorophyll traps sunlight to convert CO2 and water into glucose and oxygen.
4. False (F) — The Moon is non-luminous; it merely reflects light received from the Sun.
5. True (T) — Under standard pressure, water freezes at 0°C and boils at 100°C.
6. True (T) — Gravity is the fundamental physical force attracting masses toward Earth's center.
7. False (F) — Rubber and dry plastic are electrical insulators (non-conductors) that block electric current.
8. True (T) — An adult human skeleton is composed of exactly 206 individual bones.
SECTION F: Health & Hygiene Awareness [6 Marks]
a) 20 seconds: Scrubbing palms, back of hands, between fingers, and under nails for at least 20 seconds destroys bacterial cell walls.
b) Two crucial times for handwashing:
• 1. Before handling, preparing, or eating food.
• 2. After using the toilet / restroom (and after touching trash or sneezing).
c) Why drink boiled or filtered water: Untreated water often carries dangerous microscopic pathogens (germs, bacteria, and parasites) that cause fatal waterborne diseases like cholera, typhoid, and dysentery. Boiling kills these microorganisms completely.
d) At least 2 times: Brushing after breakfast and before bed removes food debris and prevents plaque build-up.
SECTION G: Tourist Spots Explorer [6 Marks]
a) Mount Everest (Sagarmatha) — Highest peak in the world (8,848.86 m) located in Solukhumbu, Nepal.
b) Lumbini — UNESCO World Heritage holy site and sacred birthplace of Siddhartha Gautama (Lord Buddha).
c) Mount Fuji (Fuji-san) — Japan's highest and iconic sacred volcano standing at 3,776 meters.
d) Pokhara — City of lakes in western Nepal, renowned for Phewa Lake and views of Machhapuchhre & Annapurna.
e) Tokyo Skytree — World's tallest free-standing broadcasting tower (634 m) located in Sumida, Tokyo.
f) Chitwan National Park — First national park of Nepal, famed for One-Horned Rhinos and Bengal Tigers.
