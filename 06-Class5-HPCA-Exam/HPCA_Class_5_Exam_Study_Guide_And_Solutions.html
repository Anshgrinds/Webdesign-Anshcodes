SECTION B: SHORT ANSWER QUESTIONS [15 MARKS]
1. What is a balanced diet? Name the three main food groups with one example each. (3 Marks)
Model Answer: A balanced diet is a meal that contains all essential nutrients (carbohydrates, proteins, fats, vitamins, minerals, water, and roughage) in adequate proportions required for healthy growth and body maintenance.
The three main food groups are:
  • Energy-giving foods: Carbohydrates and fats (e.g., Rice, wheat, potatoes, butter).
  • Body-building foods: Proteins (e.g., Eggs, pulses/daal, milk, fish).
  • Protective foods: Vitamins and minerals (e.g., Green leafy vegetables, citrus fruits).
2. Differentiate between Communicable Diseases and Non-Communicable Diseases with examples. (3 Marks)
Model Answer:
Communicable Diseases Non-Communicable Diseases
Spread from one person to another through air, water, food, insects, or contact. Do not spread from person to person. Caused by lifestyle, deficiency, or internal organs.
Caused by germs like bacteria, viruses, or parasites. Caused by lack of nutrients, poor habits, or aging.
Examples: Common cold, Influenza, Diarrhea, Typhoid, Dengue. Examples: Diabetes, Goitre, Asthma, High Blood Pressure, Scurvy.
3. Mention any three common ways communicable diseases spread and how we can prevent them. (3 Marks)
Model Answer:
  1. Through Air (Droplets): Germs spread through sneezing and coughing (e.g., Common cold, TB). Prevention: Cover mouth/nose when sneezing and wear masks.
  2. Through Contaminated Food and Water: Flies and dirty water spread cholera and diarrhea. Prevention: Drink boiled water and keep food covered.
  3. Through Vectors (Mosquitoes): Mosquito bites transmit dengue and malaria. Prevention: Use mosquito nets, wear long sleeves, and clear stagnant water.
4. What is passive smoking? Why is it dangerous for children? (3 Marks)
Model Answer: Passive smoking (also called secondhand smoking) means breathing in smoke that comes from another person's burning cigarette or smoke exhaled by a smoker.
It is dangerous because secondhand smoke contains the same harmful toxins, tar, and carbon monoxide. In children, it causes frequent coughs, asthma attacks, bronchitis, ear infections, and damages lung development.
5. List any three major harmful effects of alcoholism on an individual and their family. (3 Marks)
Model Answer:
  1. Damage to Internal Organs: Causes serious liver diseases (like cirrhosis), damages the nervous system, and impairs brain function.
  2. Loss of Self-Control: Causes loss of balance, slurred speech, poor judgment, and increases road accidents.
  3. Harm to Family and Society: Causes domestic arguments, domestic violence, wastes family earnings on liquor, leading to financial hardship and loss of social respect.

2. Read the short situations below and answer the questions: (2 × 2.5 = 5 Marks)

Case A: "Sunil's younger brother has watery loose stools and vomiting after eating uncovered food from a roadside vendor. He looks very tired and thirsty."

Questions & Model Answers:
a) What condition is his brother likely suffering from?
→ He is likely suffering from food poisoning / acute diarrhea and dehydration.
b) What home treatment should Sunil's parents give him immediately?
→ Give him freshly prepared Oral Rehydration Solution (ORS / Jeevan Jal) or boiled water with a pinch of salt and sugar, along with clean fluids like rice water to replace lost body water and minerals. If symptoms continue, visit a health post.

Case B: "In the school playground, an older student shows a cigarette to Aarav and says: 'Take a puff, it makes you look like a hero! Don't be scared!'"

Questions & Model Answers:
a) What kind of pressure is Aarav facing?
→ Negative peer pressure.
b) How should Aarav handle this situation safely and smartly?
→ Aarav must look straight at the boy, say "NO, smoking destroys lungs and I will never smoke" firmly, walk away to a safe place immediately, and report the incident to a teacher or principal.
3. "Prevention is always better than cure." Write five golden rules to maintain good personal health and stay disease-free. (5 Marks)
Model Answer:
  1. Eat a Balanced Diet: Eat fresh home-cooked food rich in fruits, green vegetables, pulses, and milk; limit oily junk food and street food.
  2. Drink Safe Water: Always drink boiled or filtered water to protect against cholera, diarrhea, and typhoid.
  3. Practice Good Personal Hygiene: Wash hands thoroughly with soap and water before meals and after using the toilet; brush teeth twice daily.
  4. Stay Physically Active: Exercise daily, play outdoor sports, get 8–9 hours of restful sleep, and sit with good posture.
  5. Stay Away from Harmful Habits: Say an absolute NO to smoking, alcohol, and drugs; avoid places filled with cigarette smoke.
